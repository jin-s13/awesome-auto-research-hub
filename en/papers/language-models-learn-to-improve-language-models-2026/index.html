Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • LM2LM framework that trains LLMs with reinforcement learning to generate effective language-model improvement proposals.
  • Uses a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records in context, as a scalable surrogate reward model.
  • Periodically executes high-ranked current-policy proposals in real environments and adds their measured outcomes to the RWM context to maintain feedback reliability under distribution shift.

Methodology

LM2LM trains an LLM policy with RL to propose LM improvements, using a frozen RWM to predict proposal gains from real experimental records as reward instead of running full model training for every proposal. To counter reward-model unreliability, high-ranked current-policy proposals are periodically executed and their measured outcomes are added to the RWM context. The method is evaluated across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments with real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks.

Key Results

Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost. LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

Limitations

  • Training with real feedback is expensive: RL requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, potentially consuming hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours if each requires a full model-training run.
  • Surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts.
