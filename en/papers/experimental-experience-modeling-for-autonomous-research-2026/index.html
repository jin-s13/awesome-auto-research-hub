Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Experimental Experience Modeling (EEM), a framework for informed experimental decisions by acquiring, reusing, and accumulating experimental experience.
  • Extraction and distillation of decision-relevant records from earlier experimental trajectories into a reusable experience library.
  • Retrieval and sufficiency assessment of historical experience to decide whether a candidate direction warrants further investment.
  • Targeted, low-cost pilot experiments to acquire missing decision-relevant experience on demand when historical experience is insufficient.
  • Iterative accumulation by distilling new experimental outcomes back into the experience library so it continually grows.

Methodology

The paper introduces Experimental Experience Modeling (EEM), which extracts decision-relevant records from prior experimental trajectories, distills them into reusable experience, and organizes them in an experience library. For a new experimental decision, EEM retrieves relevant historical experience, assesses whether it sufficiently supports a decision, and if not, runs a targeted low-cost pilot experiment to acquire missing experience before deciding on full-scale evaluation. The resulting outcomes are then distilled back into the library for iterative accumulation.

Key Results

Experiments on autonomous research benchmarks show that EEM improves research performance while reducing model interaction overhead, demonstrating the value of reusing accumulated experience and acquiring additional experience only when needed.
