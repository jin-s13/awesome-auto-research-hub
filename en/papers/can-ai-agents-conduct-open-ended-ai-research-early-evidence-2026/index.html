Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Shadow evaluations: a new evaluation framework where AI agents are tasked with answering the central open-ended research question of a high-quality unpublished paper, and the original authors grade the agent's output.
  • Identification of five recurring failure modes of AI agents in open-ended research: poor judgment about the bar for publishable research, uncreative responses to shortcomings in research design, ineffective backtracking from dead ends, poor resource awareness, and instruction drift.

Methodology

The authors propose shadow evaluations, where a frontier AI agent is given the central open-ended research question from an unpublished high-quality paper, along with six days and thousands of dollars of compute, and the original paper authors grade the agent's output. Two case studies were conducted using submissions to NeurIPS 2026; a robustness check used a second model and scaffold.

Key Results

The agents completed all engineering tasks autonomously but failed to make substantial progress on the research questions, resulting in unambiguous rejection by the authors. Five recurring failure modes were identified, which were reproduced in a robustness check with a different model and scaffold.

Limitations

  • Small sample size limited to two case studies from a single conference.
  • Robustness check only used one additional model and scaffold.
  • Author grading may introduce subjectivity into the evaluation.
