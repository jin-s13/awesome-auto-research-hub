Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Proposes the Large Knowledge Model (LKM), a growing, agent-native knowledge foundation for representing scientific knowledge across disciplines.
  • Organizes scientific literature into reasoning graphs with claims as core nodes and explicit reasoning chains showing how premises and evidence support conclusions.
  • Creates persistent, addressable, source-grounded knowledge objects with cross-paper links that organize aligned question, workflow, and evidence views.
  • Supports incremental extension of the knowledge foundation from newly extracted papers while preserving existing object identities.
  • Develops an agent-native, reasoning-aware scientific retrieval system that retrieves claims together with their reasoning chains and sources.

Methodology

The authors propose the Large Knowledge Model (LKM) to structure scientific literature as reasoning graphs, with claims as central nodes linked to reasoning chains, sources, and cross-paper question/workflow/evidence views. They build an agent-native retrieval system on this foundation that returns claims with their reasoning chains and sources. Evaluation uses benchmarks including SciFact-Open, ScholarQABench, ChemBench, PubMedQA, and SciBench to measure evidence retrieval, citation fidelity, and answer accuracy.

Key Results

Agents using LKM retrieve nearly double the known supporting and contradicting evidence on SciFact-Open (818 versus 443 claim-paper pairs), raise citation F1 on ScholarQABench by more than 5 points, and improve a fixed answering model by 9.3, 4.2, and 14.7 points over no retrieval on ChemBench, PubMedQA, and SciBench.
