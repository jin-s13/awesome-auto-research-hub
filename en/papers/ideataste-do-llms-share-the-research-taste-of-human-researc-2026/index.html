IdeaTaste: Do LLMs Share the Research Taste of Human Researchers?

arXiv 2026 64.6 method

TLDR

Introduces IdeaTaste, a benchmark measuring how well LLM-generated research proposals match future community work, finding they reach only 26% citation and 18% idea matching.

Reasoning

The paper presents a novel, large-scale benchmark for evaluating research taste in LLMs, with expert review and multiple models/fields. Its weakness is that the evaluation focuses on proposal-text matching rather than real-world research execution or outcomes.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces IdeaTaste, a benchmark that scores LLMs by how closely research proposals written from a 2025 literature snapshot match publications from the same communities in 2026.
  • Evaluates both citation matching and idea matching, distinguishing broad engagement with community directions from concrete research approaches.
  • Provides a large-scale comparison across six contemporary language models and four research areas, covering more than 90,000 proposals.
  • Includes blinded expert review comparing AI proposals to 2026 papers on dimensions such as presentation and expected success.
  • Releases an open, continuously updated leaderboard for benchmarking AI proposals against future publication cycles.

Methodology

IdeaTaste asks LLMs to write research proposals from a literature snapshot frozen at 2025 and scores how closely those proposals match what the same communities published in 2026. It evaluates six contemporary language models across four research areas spanning established to emerging fields, generating more than 90,000 proposals. The benchmark measures citation matching and idea matching, and also uses blinded expert review to compare AI proposals against 2026 papers.

Key Results

LLM agents reached about 26% of the human level on citation matching and only 18% on idea matching. They could cite as diversely as a community and take up its tasks and methods, but rarely arrived at the concrete approaches and research plans the community pursued; richer field context and larger populations did not substantially close the gap.

Limitations

  • The study covers only six contemporary language models and four research areas.
  • Evaluation is tied to a single 2025 literature snapshot and 2026 publication cycle, so generalization to other cycles or fields is not established.
  • The benchmark focuses on research proposals and their citation and idea overlap, not all aspects of research taste such as reading choices or execution.
