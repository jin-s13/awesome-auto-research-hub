Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Surveys automated research evaluation across six targets: literature synthesis, research ideation, executable workflows, scholarly writing and communication, automatic peer review, and end-to-end research.
  • Compares evaluation designs along task construction, evidence sources, evaluators, and scoring procedures.
  • Synthesizes three recurring evaluation lessons: output checks, process checks, and human studies are complementary; evaluator calibration is property-specific; resource budgets and attempt selection affect performance interpretation.
  • Identifies diagnostic evaluation designs and translates findings into reporting and audit recommendations for specific evaluation settings.

Methodology

This is a survey paper that reviews and organizes existing benchmarks and studies on evaluating automated research systems. It examines six research task areas and compares their evaluation designs in terms of task construction, evidence sources, evaluators, and scoring procedures. The synthesis focuses on evaluation design and evidence rather than introducing new experimental results or datasets.

Key Results

The survey highlights three recurring lessons: output checks, process checks, and human studies provide complementary information; evaluator calibration is specific to the property being assessed; and resource budgets and attempt selection are integral to interpreting performance comparisons. It also identifies diagnostic evaluation designs and documented gaps in supporting evidence.

Limitations

  • The abstract mentions documented gaps in supporting evidence but does not enumerate them.
  • No explicit limitations of the survey methodology or the reviewed evaluations are stated in the abstract.
