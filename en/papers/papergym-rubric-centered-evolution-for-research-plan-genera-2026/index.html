Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • PaperGym turns each research paper into a complete training environment by exploiting paper structure: questions are synthesized from the research goal and background, while criteria are derived from the method and experiments.
  • This separation reduces criterion leakage to 3.7%, compared with 11.90% to 34.10% in existing datasets, limiting reward hacking by paraphrase.
  • A rubric-centered training schedule uses the rubric twice: first as privileged context for an OPSD self-teacher, then as the reward signal for GRPO.
  • The work releases the pipeline, the 20,000-instance corpus PaperGym-20k, and the benchmarks PaperGym-Innov and PaperGym-Design.

Methodology

PaperGym constructs training environments from individual papers by synthesizing the question from the research goal and background and deriving evaluation criteria from the method and experiments, with criteria spanning methodological innovation and experimental design. Training follows a two-stage rubric-centered schedule in which rubrics serve first as privileged context for an OPSD self-teacher and then as the reward for GRPO. The approach is evaluated on Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B against supervised fine-tuning, either stage alone, the reverse ordering, and RubricHub Science, using five-benchmark averages, three-way win rates, and ResearchQA.

Key Results

Criterion leakage falls to 3.7%, versus 11.90% to 34.10% in existing datasets. The proposed schedule improves five-benchmark averages by +5.6, +5.0, and +4.8 points across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B; models trained on PaperGym-20k win 58.1% of three-way comparisons against 28.2% for RubricHub Science, and the trained Qwen3-8B reaches 73.48 on ResearchQA, above Kimi K2.6.
