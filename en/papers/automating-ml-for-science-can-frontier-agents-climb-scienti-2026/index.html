Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces Agent4Sci, a long-horizon testbed spanning 20 scientific ML benchmarks across 11 domains for evaluating automated improvement of published best models.
  • Shifts evaluation from leaderboard ranking to analyzing what kinds of progress agents make under in-the-wild conditions and where they fall short.
  • Uses trajectory analysis to identify recurring agent limitations: novelty miscalibration, resource under-utilization, and rapid collapse into local engineering sweeps.
  • Proposes lightweight plug-in skills for research ideation, experiment management, and scheduled checklists that improve frontier agent performance and intervention diversity.

Methodology

Agent4Sci evaluates frontier coding agents on improving published best models across 20 scientific ML benchmarks in 11 domains under in-the-wild conditions. The study measures mean relative improvement and academic misconduct flags, and analyzes agent trajectories to characterize progress and failures. It also tests the effect of adding plug-in skills for research ideation, experiment management, and scheduled checklists, comparing Claude Opus and Codex.

Key Results

Frontier agents improved the published best on every benchmark, with Claude Opus reaching 10.4% mean relative improvement and no run flagged for academic misconduct, though gains came almost entirely from ML-engineering tricks. Plug-in skills raised Claude Opus from 10.4% to 20.1% and Codex from 4.4% to 8.1%, with more diverse, domain-relevant interventions.

Limitations

  • Gains come almost entirely from ML-engineering tricks rather than research-level insight.
  • Trajectory analysis shows novelty miscalibration.
  • Trajectory analysis shows resource under-utilization.
  • Trajectory analysis shows rapid collapse into local engineering sweeps.
