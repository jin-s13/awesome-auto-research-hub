Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end physics research benchmark with 56 tasks authored by 42 physicists based on their own research.
  • Covers nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics, requiring full research workflows rather than isolated subtasks.
  • Evaluates both research correctness and publishability by combining deterministic verifiers with expert-authored rubrics.
  • Identifies frontier agents' primary weaknesses as insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition, rather than physics knowledge gaps or execution errors.

Methodology

FrontierPhysics consists of 56 tasks created by 42 physicists from their own research, spanning nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics. Each task requires agents to review literature, develop a research plan, conduct research and experiments, and produce structured outputs and scientific manuscripts. Evaluation combines deterministic verifiers with expert-authored rubrics, and 22 models in 26 configurations were tested with manual review of agent trajectories.

Key Results

Across 22 models in 26 configurations, no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts. Manual review of agent trajectories shows frontier agents' main weaknesses are insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition, rather than gaps in physics knowledge or execution errors.
