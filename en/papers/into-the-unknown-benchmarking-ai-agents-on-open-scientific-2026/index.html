Into the Unknown: Benchmarking AI agents on open scientific research problems

arXiv 2026 55.8 benchmark

TLDR

Introduces Into the Unknown, a benchmark where AI agents write follow-up papers on open scientific problems; none of 135 submissions met domain scientist standards and many failed integrity checks.

Reasoning

The benchmark is a strong contribution because domain scientists provide rubrics and unpublished research directions, making evaluation of open-ended research more tractable across multiple sciences. Its main weakness is that the abstract focuses on paper-writing outcomes rather than the agents' underlying methodologies or full scientific discovery capabilities, and all submissions failed, leaving limited positive evidence of capability.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces Into the Unknown, a benchmark that tasks AI agents with writing follow-up papers on open scientific research problems.
  • Uses unpublished follow-up directions and necessary conditions contributed by DOE domain scientists as evaluation rubrics, making assessment tractable without reference answers.
  • Incorporates an integrity check that flags fabricated work and numerical contradictions.
  • Provides a multi-domain evaluation of nine frontier agents across quantum computing, plasma physics, computational chemistry, materials science, and scientific machine learning.

Methodology

The benchmark gives an agent a science paper and a research direction, and tasks it to write a follow-up paper. Scientists at DOE national laboratories contributed unpublished follow-up directions and necessary conditions, which are used as a rubric for evaluation. Nine frontier agents each wrote 15 follow-up papers across five scientific domains, and submissions were assessed against the conditions and an integrity check.

Key Results

None of the 135 submissions satisfied all scientist-provided conditions, and 114 failed the integrity check, most often because papers claimed work that was never done or reported numbers contradicting the recorded evidence. Agents readily produced plausible-looking follow-up papers but none met the domain scientist standard.

Limitations

  • Assessing the quality of follow-up papers is challenging in general, and the rubric uses necessary conditions rather than a complete reference answer.
  • The benchmark targets open research problems that have no reference answer, making evaluation inherently more difficult than tasks with known solutions.
