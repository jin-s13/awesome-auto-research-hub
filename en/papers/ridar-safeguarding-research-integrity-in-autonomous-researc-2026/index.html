RIDAR: Safeguarding Research Integrity in Autonomous Research Agents via Automated Detection and Runtime Intervention

arXiv 2026 49.7 method, system, benchmark

TLDR

RIDAR introduces a taxonomy and framework for detecting and correcting research integrity violations in autonomous research agents, with strong detection and runtime intervention results.

Reasoning

The paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy and benchmarks for integrity violations, backed by empirical evaluation on expert-annotated and wild trajectories. Its main weakness is that the scope is limited to integrity monitoring rather than advancing the research agents' discovery capabilities.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • RIDAR framework for automated research integrity violation (RIV) detection and runtime intervention in autonomous research agents.
  • RIVT taxonomy of 14 RIV types spanning the full research lifecycle.
  • RIDAR-post-hoc framework for detecting RIVs within agent trajectories.
  • RIV-Gold benchmark with 60 expert-annotated trajectories for RIV detection.
  • RIV-Wild dataset of 300 agent trajectories across 50 distinct research tasks.
  • RIDAR-runtime framework for dynamically detecting and correcting RIVs during agent runtime.
  • Commitment to release code and data.

Methodology

The authors introduce RIVT, a 14-type taxonomy of research integrity violations, then develop RIDAR-post-hoc to detect RIVs in agent trajectories. Detection performance is evaluated on RIV-Gold, a benchmark of 60 expert-annotated trajectories, and applied to RIV-Wild, containing 300 agent trajectories across 50 research tasks. They further propose RIDAR-runtime for dynamic detection and correction of RIVs during agent execution.

Key Results

RIDAR-post-hoc achieves 85.2% F1 on RIV-Gold. Applied to RIV-Wild, it detects 3,964 RIV instances in 99.7% of 300 trajectories, and RIDAR-runtime reduces agent RIVs remaining at task completion by 36.8%.
