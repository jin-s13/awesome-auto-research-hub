Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces EmbodiedLab, a benchmark for embodied agents that 'act to know', where interaction is the means to acquire knowledge rather than only to change a target physical state.
  • Uses quantitative science experiments with per-instance resampled parameters so the answer cannot be known in advance but can still be scored exactly.
  • Provides 30 experiments across five disciplines on 14 apparatus platforms in a physics-based simulated laboratory where agents choose their own procedures and read instruments through cameras.
  • Defines an evaluation protocol that checks what the agent came to know from readings recorded during the episode and the physical state required by the task, with every experiment verified to be solvable.
  • Benchmarks six frontier models under native control, added perception and motion planning, and distilled manipulation skills, revealing failure patterns and skill impacts.

Methodology

EmbodiedLab is a physics-based simulated laboratory benchmark containing 30 quantitative science experiments across five disciplines and 14 apparatus platforms. Agents receive an objective and the apparatus, choose their own procedure, and read instruments through cameras, with system parameters resampled per instance so results cannot be known in advance but are exactly scorable. Six frontier models were evaluated in their developers' coding agents under three conditions: native observation and control, added perception and motion planning, and manipulation skills distilled from the strongest agents.

Key Results

With native control, even the strongest agent succeeds on less than half of the experiments. For every agent, most failures arise in interacting with the apparatus, and manipulation skills help weaker agents more than added perception and planning.

Limitations

  • The benchmark is implemented in a physics-based simulated laboratory, not a physical real-world laboratory.
  • Evaluation is limited to six frontier models used through their developers' coding agents, so other agent architectures or real-robot deployments are not covered.
  • The abstract does not provide explicit limitations or detailed failure analysis beyond the aggregate finding that apparatus interaction is the main failure source.
