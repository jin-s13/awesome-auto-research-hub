REE: A Scientific Research Entrance Exam for AI Agents

arXiv 2026 58.7 method

TLDR

REE is a benchmark of 100 open-ended research problems evaluating AI agents' ability to iteratively improve starter solutions over expert baselines across five scientific areas.

Reasoning

The paper introduces a well-designed benchmark with expert-reviewed problems and a unified scoring framework, and it provides empirical results for frontier agents. Weaknesses include limited detail on problem selection and potential constraints of the 12-hour setup, which may affect generalizability.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces REE (Research Entrance Exam), a benchmark of 100 open-ended scientific research problems spanning five broad scientific areas and 23 domains, with every problem drawn from active research and individually reviewed by domain experts.
  • Frames evaluation as iterative improvement: an agent has up to 12 hours to improve a working starter solution, scored on a common scale against an established expert solution evaluated under comparable conditions.
  • Provides a unified framework for evaluating autonomous scientific research that captures both improvements over established methods and the research processes behind them.

Methodology

REE consists of 100 expert-reviewed open-ended research problems from active research across five scientific areas and 23 domains. Agents are given a working starter solution and up to 12 hours to improve it, after which solutions are scored on a common scale against established expert solutions evaluated under comparable conditions. The study evaluates four frontier agents, including Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol.

Key Results

Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol outperform the expert solutions on 86% and 81% of problems, respectively, across all five scientific areas. Both agents surpass expert solutions within the first two hours on average, and their best solutions mostly tailor or combine established methods.
